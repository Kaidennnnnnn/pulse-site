How many shares should I buy?

Decide what you are willing to lose first. The size of the trade follows from that and from where your stop-loss sits.

The formula

Units = (account x risk %) / (entry price - stop price). With £10,000, 1% risk, an entry at £50 and a stop at £47, you risk £100, each share risks £3, so you buy 33 shares.

Why size from the stop

A wide stop means a smaller position, and a tight stop a bigger one, but the money at risk stays the same. That is how professionals keep every loss small and planned, however volatile the market is.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing the size first and the stop second, so the stop lands wherever it happens to fit.
  • Risking more after a win, or trying to win a loss back.
  • Forgetting that a gap can carry price through the stop, so the loss can exceed the plan.
