Pulse / Learn

Learn to trade, free.

The first three levels of the Pulse Academy as plain guides, written for complete beginners. 16 lessons, no sign-up. The app has 686 in total.

Level 1: Market foundations

What you're actually trading

  • What is a stock?When you buy one Apple share, you own a tiny slice of a company worth trillions. Why does that slice change price every second?
  • Indices and ETFsWhen the news says "the market fell 2%", which market? Usually an index like the S&P 500.
  • Crypto, currencies and commoditiesBitcoin trades on Christmas Day. Gold reacts to interest rates. The pound moves on Bank of England decisions. Different markets, different rules.
  • Bid, ask and the spreadEvery trade you open starts slightly at a loss. Here's why.
  • Market hours and gapsA stock closes at $100. Overnight, results disappoint. It opens at $88. Your stop at $95 fills at $88. What happened?
  • News, data and why prices jumpA chart can look perfect and one headline can still send the price straight through it. Knowing when news is due is part of reading any chart.

Level 2: Orders and execution

How a trade really happens

  • Market and limit ordersTwo ways to buy: right now at whatever the price is, or only at your price. Each has a cost.
  • Stop orders and stop-lossesThe single most important order in trading is the one that gets you out.
  • Trailing stops and bracketsHow do you let a winner run without giving all the profit back?
  • Long and short: the basicsYou can make money when prices fall. But the risk works differently.
  • Fees, FX and leverageA strategy that makes 5% a year can lose money once costs are counted.

Level 3: Reading charts

Candles, trends and levels

  • CandlesticksOne candle tells you four prices and who won the battle that day.
  • TimeframesThe same stock can be in an uptrend, a downtrend and a range at the same time, depending on the chart you open.
  • Trends"The trend is your friend" is the oldest saying in trading. Here's how to actually see one.
  • Support and resistancePrices remember. Levels where buyers or sellers stepped in before tend to matter again.
  • VolumePrice tells you what happened. Volume tells you how much conviction was behind it.
