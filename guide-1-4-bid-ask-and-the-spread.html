Bid, ask and the spread

Every trade you open starts slightly at a loss. Here's why.

Level 1: Market foundations · 4 min read · Fundamentals

Two prices

At any moment there's a bid (the highest price a buyer will pay) and an ask (the lowest price a seller will accept). The gap is the spread.

Who pays it

Buy at the ask, sell at the bid. If you bought and sold instantly you'd lose the spread. That's a cost on every round trip.

Liquidity

Busy markets like Apple or EUR/USD have tiny spreads. Small or obscure assets can have wide spreads that eat your profits.

In Pulse

Paper trades fill at a simulated bid or ask, with extra slippage in fast markets, so your results stay realistic.

Remember this

  • The spread is the gap between the bid and the ask.
  • You buy at the ask and sell at the bid, so every round trip costs the spread.
  • Busy markets have tiny spreads; obscure assets can have spreads that eat your profit.

Test yourself

1. You buy at...

Buyers pay the ask, the lowest price sellers accept.

2. The spread is...

Spread = ask minus bid.

3. Where are spreads usually widest?

Fewer traders means fewer competing quotes, so wider spreads.
