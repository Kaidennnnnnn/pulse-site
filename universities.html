Pulse / Universities

Markets literacy for every student.

Pulse is a structured course in how markets work, with risk-free practice built in. We are looking for a small number of London universities and business schools to run a free founding pilot with one group of students.

Arrange a conversationHow a pilot works

Pulse is an early-stage company. We have no university partners yet, and this page says plainly what exists today and what does not.

The problem

Students are already trading. Few have been taught how.

Trading apps have made it easy for a student to buy shares, crypto or leveraged products from a phone. The teaching has not kept up. Finance students get terminals and desk simulations. Everyone else gets social media.

Risk comes last, or never

Most beginner content starts with what to buy. Pulse starts with what can go wrong: position size, stop-losses, leverage and why most active traders lose money.

Practice is separate from teaching

A simulator on its own rewards the biggest gamble. A course on its own is forgotten. Pulse joins the two, so each idea is practised straight after it is taught.

Specialist tools reach few students

Trading rooms and terminal certificates serve finance cohorts well. A history or engineering student who wants to understand markets has no equivalent.

What Pulse is

A full course, in five-minute lessons.

Everything below exists today and can be seen on this site or in the app.

Course
74 courses and 686 lessons, about 92 hours, from "What is a stock?" to options, macro, valuation and building a tested trading plan. See the syllabus.
Lesson format
One idea at a time, a question after every couple of ideas, and an answer that always explains itself. Lessons take 4 to 11 minutes. See a lesson.
Practice
A paper-trading account with virtual money and realistic costs: spreads, slippage, fees and margin rules. No real money is involved at any point.
Help at the decision
As a student sets up a practice trade, Pulse points out a missing stop-loss, a trade larger than their own risk limit or a trade placed straight after a loss.
Habits, not profit
After a handful of trades, the journal compares a student's early habits with their recent ones: stops used, risk per trade and plans followed.
Assessment
2,698 quiz questions with explanations, a 12-question exam for each level with an 80% pass mark, and a completion certificate in the app.
For a cohort
A study group code from the tutor, a study mode that hides buy and sell signals, and a progress report (lessons, quiz scores, exams, practice habits) that each student can choose to send to their tutor.
For a seminar
A class challenge that runs in any browser: everyone trades the same simulated market from one link, with no sign-up. Set one up.
Open resources
16 beginner guides, a glossary of 1,114 terms, risk calculators and a quiz, free on this site for any student. Open the guides.
Who gains what

Useful to students. Low risk for you.

What students gain

  • A real grounding. How shares, funds, currencies and crypto work, how orders are filled and what things cost.
  • Risk habits before real money. Position sizing, stop-losses and a written plan, practised until they are routine.
  • Protection from scams. A full course on pump-and-dump schemes, fake brokers, signal groups and recovery fraud.
  • Vocabulary for interviews. The terms used on a trading floor and in the financial press, each explained in a sentence.
  • Something to show. Level exams and completion certificates for each course finished.

What your institution gains

  • Reach beyond the finance cohort. A way to offer markets literacy to students on any degree.
  • A financial wellbeing offer. Education that is frank about losses, for students who may already be trading.
  • Nothing to procure or install. No licence, no software on university machines and no cost for a pilot.
  • No student data to govern. Pulse has no accounts today, so a pilot needs no data-sharing agreement.
  • A say in what we build. A founding partner shapes the cohort tools and the inter-university cup described below.
The founding pilot

One group. Four to six weeks. No cost.

We would rather run one small pilot well than sign a large agreement badly. This is the whole process.

  1. A twenty-minute call

    We show the app and the session plan. You tell us about your students. Either side can stop here.

  2. Choose one group

    An investment society, a careers programme or one seminar group. Twenty to a hundred students is ideal.

  3. Run it

    Students follow the beginner path at their own pace and meet for one class challenge. We answer their questions directly.

  4. Review together

    We send you a short written report: what students used, what they said and what we would change. Then you decide what, if anything, comes next.

What we provide

  • Access to the app for every student in the group, free.
  • A four-session plan with the lessons, the class challenge and discussion questions.
  • A one-page student guide and a short briefing for your contact.
  • Feedback questions at the start, the middle and the end, and the written report.
  • Direct support by email for students and staff throughout.

What we ask of you

  • One named contact.
  • One message to the chosen group of students.
  • Half an hour at the end to tell us honestly what you thought.

We do not ask for payment, exclusivity, student data or the use of your name.

What is ready, and what is not.

You will ask, so here it is in one place.

  • The browser simulator, class challenge, guides, glossary, calculators and quiz on this site.
  • A four-session plan for a seminar or a society.

Questions institutions ask

Straight answers.

Is Pulse a broker, and does it give financial advice?

No. Pulse is an educational app. It never places real trades, holds no client money and gives no personal financial advice. Every trade in Pulse is a practice trade with virtual money. The app says so at the first launch and on every page of this site.

Does it encourage students to gamble or to trade real money?

It is built to do the opposite. The course teaches that most active retail traders lose money, and the first lessons cover risk before strategy. The order ticket points out a missing stop-loss or a position that is too large, and the journal reports habits such as stops used and risk taken, not profit. Pulse has no link to a broker and no button that opens a real account.

What student data do you collect?

Today, none. Pulse has no accounts. Lessons, practice trades and the journal are stored on the student's own iPhone, and the browser simulator stores nothing on a server. A pilot can therefore start without a data-sharing agreement. If a pilot needs cohort reporting, we would agree in writing what is collected, and it would be opt-in for each student.

What does it cost?

A pilot is free for the institution and for its students. We would only discuss a paid arrangement after a pilot, based on what proved useful. The app is free for individual students while it is in its early version.

Which devices does it work on?

The full app is for iPhone. The beginner guides, the paper-trading simulator, the class challenge, the calculators and the quiz on this website work in any modern browser, on a laptop, tablet or Android phone. There is no Android app yet, and we say so to students at the start of a pilot.

Is the app available now?

Pulse is in an early test version and is not yet on the App Store. For a pilot, students would install it through Apple's TestFlight, which needs no payment and no account with us. The website tools are available today.

What evidence is there that it works?

We have no outcome data yet, and we will not pretend otherwise. Published research suggests that financial education on its own changes behaviour only a little, which is why Pulse puts help at the moment of decision and measures habits. A pilot is how we would produce the first evidence, and we would share the results with you in full, including what did not work.

How much staff time does a pilot need?

Very little. We suggest one named contact, one message to the chosen group of students and a short conversation at the end. We supply the session plan, the student guide and the feedback questions.

Can it sit inside a module, or is it extra-curricular?

Either. The simplest start is a student society or a careers programme. For a module, the four-session plan and the class challenge can be used in seminars. The certificates in the app mark course completion. They are not accredited and carry no academic credit.

How is the content checked?

Lessons are written in plain English and each one that quotes a rule or a limit that can change is on a dated list that we recheck before each release. Lessons are in English. The interface and the glossary are in twelve languages, and those translations have not yet been reviewed by native speakers. If your academics find an error, we will correct it and tell you.

Does the app show buy and sell signals?

It shows educational signals from a transparent scoring model, with the reasons for each one, so that students learn how such calls are built and where they fail. They are general information, they can be wrong, and the app says so. In a pilot we would ask students to switch on study mode, which hides the signals altogether.

Is the artificial intelligence switched on?

Not in the early test version. Lessons, practice and the journal work without it. If the AI teacher is switched on later, we will tell partner institutions first and explain what is sent to it.

Is it suitable for students under 18?

Pulse is designed for adults. For anyone under 18, your own policy and the App Store age rating apply.

Do you ask for exclusivity or for use of our name?

No. We ask for neither. We would not use your institution's name or logo anywhere without written permission.
