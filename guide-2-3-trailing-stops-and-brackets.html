Trailing stops and brackets

How do you let a winner run without giving all the profit back?

Level 2: Orders and execution · 6 min read · Risk

Trailing stop

A stop that moves with price by a fixed distance. If price rises $10, the stop rises $10; if price falls, the stop stays put.

Choosing the distance

Too tight and normal wiggles stop you out. A common choice is 2 to 3 times the ATR, the asset's average daily range.

Bracket orders

Entry, stop-loss and take-profit placed together. The moment you enter, your exit plan is live.

In Pulse

Every order ticket lets you attach a stop and a target, and turn the stop into a trailing stop.

Remember this

  • A trailing stop follows price by a fixed distance and never moves back.
  • About 2 to 3 times the ATR is a common trailing distance.
  • A bracket order puts your entry, stop and target in place together.

Test yourself

1. If price falls, a trailing stop on a long trade...

It only ratchets in the trade's favour.

2. A bracket order contains...

All three parts of the plan at once.

3. A trailing distance of 0.2 ATR will probably...

It's inside normal daily movement.
