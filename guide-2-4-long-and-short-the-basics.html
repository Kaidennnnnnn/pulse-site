Long and short: the basics

You can make money when prices fall. But the risk works differently.

Level 2: Orders and execution · 5 min read · Strategy

Going long

Buy first, sell later. You profit if price rises. The most you can lose is what you paid, if the price goes to zero.

Going short

Sell first (with borrowed shares), buy back later. You profit if price falls. Because price can rise without limit, so can your loss.

Costs of shorting

Borrowing fees, paying any dividends, and in the UK usually CFDs or spread bets with leverage. Shorts need tighter discipline.

Same rules, mirrored

Long: stop below support, target at resistance. Short: stop above resistance, target at support.

Remember this

  • Going long profits when price rises and your loss is limited to what you paid.
  • Going short profits when price falls, but the loss has no upper limit.
  • Short rules mirror long rules: stop above resistance, target at support.

Test yourself

1. You short at $100 and buy back at $80. Your result per share is...

Sold at 100, bought at 80: you keep the $20 difference.

2. Why is shorting riskier?

There's no ceiling on a rising price.

3. On a short, the stop-loss goes...

You're wrong if price rises, so the stop sits above.
