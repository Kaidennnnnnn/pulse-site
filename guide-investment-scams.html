How to spot an investment scam

The costume changes every year. The levers do not. Here are the signs, the two checks that take five minutes, and what to do if you have already paid.

Staying safe · 7 min read · for UK beginners · checked 21 November 2026

The four levers every scam pulls

  • A return that is too good. "2% a month, guaranteed" sounds modest. Compounded, it is about 27% a year with no bad months, which no real market hands anyone. Any promised return is the warning.
  • Pressure. "The window closes tonight." Real opportunities survive a day of research.
  • Secrecy. "Don't mention it to your bank." Banks stop a lot of fraud; scammers know it.
  • Borrowed trust. A friend's recommendation, a famous face in an advert, a slick website, a "regulated" logo. None of these is proof. The friend may have been scammed first.

How they reach you

A message on Instagram, TikTok or WhatsApp from an "account manager" or a "signals group". A reply under a finance video offering to help. A dating-app match who, weeks in, mentions their crypto returns. A cold call about a pension review. A polished app that shows your balance growing, until the day you try to withdraw and there is a "release fee". That fee is the surest sign of all: a real broker never charges you to take your own money out.

Two checks that take five minutes

  1. Look the firm up yourself. Use the Financial Conduct Authority's Firm Checker, reached by typing the address, never through a link the firm sent you. Check that the firm exists, that it has permission for the service it is offering, and that the phone number and website on the register match the ones you were given. Scammers copy real firms' names and numbers (a "clone firm"), so matching the contact details is the part that catches them.
  2. Check the Warning List. The FCA keeps a list of firms it has been warned about. It is not complete, so a firm being absent proves nothing; a firm being present settles it.

Being authorised is still not a guarantee of a good investment, only that the firm is regulated and that you may have access to the ombudsman and the compensation scheme if the firm fails. If you cannot find a firm, the FCA's consumer helpline is 0800 111 6768.

If you have already paid

Stop paying, including any "fee" to release funds; that is the same scam continuing. Tell your bank at once; they may be able to recall a transfer. Report it to Action Fraud (0300 123 2040) in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, or to Police Scotland. Keep every message. Expect a follow-up contact from a "recovery agent" who can get your money back for a fee. That is the same people again.

Why a trading course teaches this first

People are scammed in the gap between wanting to invest and knowing how. Close the gap and the pitch stops working: once you know what a realistic return looks like, "2% a month guaranteed" reads as a joke. The Pulse Academy has a full course on recognising investment fraud, and the free lessons on spreads and fees and leverage show what real trading costs, which is the best inoculation there is. Checked on 21 November 2026 against the FCA's own guidance.

Remember this

  • Promised returns, pressure, secrecy and borrowed trust: the four levers of every scam.
  • A fee to withdraw your own money is a scam, every time.
  • Look the firm up on the FCA Firm Checker yourself and match the contact details. Then check the Warning List.
  • If you have paid: stop, tell your bank, report to Action Fraud, and ignore anyone offering recovery for a fee.
