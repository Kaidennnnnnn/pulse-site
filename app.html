The iPhone app

This is what a lesson looks like.

Real screens from the app, in the order a new learner meets them. It works like a language app: one path, short steps, instant feedback. The only difference is the subject.

Early test version. App 1.0, website built 24 November 2026. The app is not on the App Store yet, so this page shows what it looks like.

Step 1 · Your path

One path, one next step

The app opens on a single winding path of short lessons. The glowing circle is your next lesson. Finish it and the next one opens. Nothing to choose, nothing to set up. There is a short placement test for people who already know the basics.

Step 2 · Starting a lesson

You are told what is coming

Every lesson begins by saying how long it takes and what it will ask of you: short ideas, quick checks, some practice, then a recap. Aurel, the guide, keeps you company. The bar at the top shows how far through you are.

Step 3 · One idea at a time

Short, and no jargon left unexplained

Each screen is one idea in plain English. Underlined words open a plain meaning when you tap them, so a beginner is never left guessing what a word means. "Explain it more simply" asks for another way of saying it.

Step 4 · Checking it stuck

A quick question after every couple of ideas

Pick an answer and tap Check. If you are stuck, you can peek at the idea the question is about. A peek still counts, it just does not add to your streak of right answers.

Step 5 · Right or wrong

Instant feedback, and always the reason

A right answer gets a cheer and the reason it is right. A wrong one shows the correct answer and why, with a button to review the idea. The question comes back at the end of the lesson so you get a second go.

Step 6 · Play with it

Move the sliders and watch the numbers change

Some lessons turn a formula into a toy. Drag a slider, see what leverage or a fee does to your money, and try to reach the goal. If you get stuck, Show me moves the sliders to the answer.

Step 7 · Practice

Short games to make it stick

The Practice tab turns what you learned into quick rounds: a daily challenge, your own mistakes to fix, a review of trader words, a timed lightning round and number drills with fresh numbers each time.

Step 8 · Paper trading

Practise with pretend money on real prices

When you are ready, you can place trades in a paper account with £10,000 of pretend money. The ticket helps at the moment you decide: forget a stop-loss and it says so, with one tap to add a sensible one and a link to the lesson that explains why.
