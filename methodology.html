Pulse / How signals work

How a Pulse signal is made.

No black box. Every rating is a weighted score from seven factors, and every confidence number follows a formula you can try below.

The method in four steps

1. Seven factors, each scored

Trend, momentum, volume and flow, key levels, fundamentals, news and market context each get a score from -100 (very bearish) to +100 (very bullish), with the evidence written out in plain English.

2. Weighted by the market

The weights change with the market regime. In a range, levels matter more and trend less. In a strong uptrend, trend and momentum count for more. Factors with no data drop out and the rest are rescaled.

3. A rating, then a confidence

The composite score becomes Strong Sell to Strong Buy. Confidence is separate: it measures how much the factors agree, not how extreme the score is.

4. Marked on a track record

Every signal you see is logged and scored when its horizon ends: two days for intraday, 20 for swing, 90 for position. If past confidence ran too high, future confidence is scaled down.

    What this does not do

    A signal is general information produced by software. It does not know your finances, goals or risk tolerance, it can be wrong, and it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything. When the next price level gives a reward of less than 1.5 times the risk, the rating is softened one step toward Hold, and in high-volatility markets the suggested position size is halved. Read the risk and education notice.
