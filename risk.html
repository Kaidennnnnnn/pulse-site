Risk and education notice

Pulse is built to teach. Here is what it is, what it isn't, and the risks to understand before trading for real.

Education, not advice

Pulse teaches how markets work and lets you practise with virtual money. Nothing in the app or on this website is a personal recommendation, and Pulse is not authorised to give investment advice. Signals and AI answers are general information produced by software. They can be wrong and they do not know your finances or goals.

Your capital is at risk

The value of investments can fall as well as rise, and you may get back less than you put in. Prices can move sharply on news, outside market hours and in ways no model predicts. Past performance, including backtests and signal track records, is not a reliable guide to future results.

Leverage, margin and short selling

Leverage multiplies gains and losses. With margin, futures and some other products, you can lose money faster than you expect and, depending on the product, more than you deposited. Short selling has no limit on how far a price can rise against you, and short positions can be closed out at a bad moment.

Higher-risk products

  • Options can expire worthless, and selling options can expose you to large losses.
  • CFDs and spread bets are complex leveraged products. Brokers must publish the share of their retail clients who lose money with them, and it is usually most of them.
  • Cryptoassets are highly volatile, are largely unregulated in many countries, and you could lose all the money you put in. Scams are common.

Limits of models and backtests

Signals, grades, scanners and backtests rely on past data and simple assumptions. Backtests can look better than reality because of overfitting, missing costs and hindsight. Treat every model as one input to your own judgement.

Paper trading is not real trading

Simulated results leave out much of real trading: slippage in fast markets, partial fills, and the stress of having real money at stake. Start small if you decide to trade for real, and only with money you can afford to lose.

Funded-trader programmes

Pulse's challenges are modelled on the evaluations that funded-trader firms run. Those firms are private businesses. Many charge fees for each attempt, most attempts fail, and their rules and payouts vary. Research any firm carefully before paying.

Getting help

  • For personal advice, use a regulated financial adviser.
  • In the UK, MoneyHelper (moneyhelper.org.uk) offers free, impartial guidance on money and investing.
  • Check that a firm is authorised on the FCA Register (register.fca.org.uk) before you invest, and see the FCA's ScamSmart pages for how to spot a scam.
  • If trading is causing you stress or money worries, stop, take a break, and talk to someone you trust.
