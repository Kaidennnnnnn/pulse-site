What is a stock?

When you buy one Apple share, you own a tiny slice of a company worth trillions. Why does that slice change price every second?

Level 1: Market foundations · 4 min read · Fundamentals

Ownership

A share is a unit of ownership in a company. Owners share in its profits, through dividends or a rising share price, and in its losses.

Why prices move

A price is simply the last amount a buyer and seller agreed on. When more people want to buy than sell, buyers must pay more, and the price rises.

Long-term driver

Over years, share prices tend to follow a company's earnings. Over days, they follow news, emotion and the flow of orders.

Where they trade

Shares trade on exchanges such as the NYSE and Nasdaq in New York, the LSE in London and Euronext in Paris, each with its own opening hours.

Remember this

  • A share is a slice of ownership in a company.
  • A price is the last amount a buyer and seller agreed on.
  • Over years prices follow earnings; over days they follow news, emotion and orders.

Test yourself

1. What does owning a share give you?

A share is ownership. A loan to a company is a bond.

2. In the short term, what mostly moves prices?

Day to day, orders and sentiment dominate. Earnings matter more over years.

3. If many more people want to buy than sell, the price will usually...

Buyers must offer more to find sellers, so the price rises.
