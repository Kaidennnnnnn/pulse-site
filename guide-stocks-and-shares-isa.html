What is a Stocks and Shares ISA?

An ISA is not an investment. It is a wrapper that stops UK tax touching what is inside. Here is how the Stocks and Shares kind works, in plain English.

Tax and accounts · 7 min read · for UK beginners · checked 21 November 2026

The one-sentence version

A Stocks and Shares ISA is an account, opened with a provider, inside which you can hold shares, funds and ETFs without paying UK income tax on the dividends or capital gains tax on the profits. The ISA is the box. What you put in the box is up to you.

What the allowance means

Every UK tax year (6 April to 5 April) an adult can pay a set amount into ISAs. For 2026/27 that overall allowance is £20,000, shared across every ISA you pay into that year, whether Cash, Stocks and Shares, Innovative Finance or Lifetime. Pay £5,000 into a Cash ISA and you have £15,000 of allowance left for the rest.

Two things people get wrong. The allowance is about money paid in, not the value of the account: if your £20,000 grows to £25,000, nothing is owed and nothing counts against next year. And the allowance does not roll over: whatever you have not used by 5 April is gone.

A change has been announced for April 2027: the amount that can go into a Cash ISA is due to fall to £12,000 a year for people under 65, while the £20,000 overall limit stays. That does not reduce what can go into a Stocks and Shares ISA. Check the current figures on gov.uk before you act on them, because this is exactly the kind of rule that moves.

What you can hold inside

  • Shares listed on a recognised exchange: a FTSE 100 company, a US company, and so on.
  • Funds and ETFs: a basket of many companies bought as one thing. Most beginners' ISAs are mostly this. What is an ETF?
  • Bonds and cash, in smaller amounts, while you decide.

What you cannot do inside an ISA is as important: no leverage, no short selling, no CFDs, no crypto. An ISA is built for owning things, not for trading on margin. That is why the Pulse Academy teaches the two activities separately.

Why the tax part matters more than it sounds

Outside an ISA, dividends above the dividend allowance (£500 for 2026/27) are taxed at a rate that depends on your income tax band, and profits above the capital gains allowance are taxed when you sell. Inside an ISA, neither applies, and you do not have to report any of it on a tax return. For someone starting with small sums the saving is small too. The point is that the saving compounds: the tax you do not pay stays invested.

What it costs

Providers charge in two places: a platform fee for the account (a flat monthly amount, or a percentage of what you hold) and the cost of the investments themselves (a fund's ongoing charge, and the bid-ask spread when you buy a share). A 0.5% platform fee on £1,000 is £5 a year. A £5 monthly fee on the same £1,000 is £60 a year, or 6%. The right structure depends on how much you hold, which is why comparing fees is the first job, not the last.

Who can open one

You need to be 18 or over and resident in the UK for tax. You can pay into more than one Stocks and Shares ISA in the same tax year since the 2024 rule change, as long as the total stays inside the allowance. Transfers between providers should be done as an ISA transfer through the new provider, never by withdrawing and paying back in, because a withdrawal does not give the allowance back.

The mistakes beginners make

  • Opening the ISA and leaving the money as cash inside it for months. The wrapper does nothing until something is bought.
  • Treating the allowance as a target. £20,000 is a ceiling, not a goal.
  • Putting money in that may be needed within a year or two. Markets fall; a Stocks and Shares ISA is for money you can leave alone.
  • Confusing the ISA with the investment. "My ISA lost money" means the things inside it fell. The box is fine.

Learn the pieces properly

An ISA is only useful once you understand what you are buying. The free Pulse guides cover the basics: what a share is, indices and ETFs and why every trade starts slightly at a loss. Sources checked: gov.uk on ISAs and gov.uk on dividend tax.

Remember this

  • An ISA is a tax wrapper, not an investment. What goes inside is your choice.
  • The 2026/27 allowance is £20,000 of money paid in, shared across all your ISAs, and it does not roll over.
  • Dividends and gains inside the ISA are free of UK tax and do not go on a tax return.
  • Compare platform fees and fund charges first. A flat fee on a small balance can be expensive.
